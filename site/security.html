The list the government stopped keeping

From 2018 the Justice Department ran the China Initiative and published a register of the prosecutions it brought under it. The page was last updated on …. The Initiative was ended on 23 February 2022. The register now sits in the archive.

Nothing replaced it. No agency publishes a successor list, so after that date these cases can be found one press release at a time and counted by nobody. That is what this page is for. The register below is the government’s own, parsed from its own words, and the cases after it are the ones a reader has to already know to look for.

What this cannot tell you

Four limits, and they travel with every number above.

  • A charge is an allegation. Nothing on this page says any person named on it is guilty of anything. Roughly half the entries were announced as accusations, and an accusation is where many of them stop being documented.
  • There is no denominator. The United States graduates tens of thousands of research doctorates a year and hosts hundreds of thousands of students and scholars. A few dozen cases over four years is not a rate, and dividing one by the other would invent one.
  • The list was never complete. DOJ called these examples. It published no criteria for inclusion and no count of what it left out.
  • It stops in 2021. The frozen list cannot show a trend, and the cases since cannot be counted, because nobody enumerates them. The absence is the finding.
