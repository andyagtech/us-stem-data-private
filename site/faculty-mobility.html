Where the expertise went

Federal research money buys two things: the research, and the researcher. The research is published. The researcher can leave, and when they do the public has paid to create expertise it no longer has.

Each case below is a professor who held a federally funded appointment at a US institution and now holds one abroad. Every figure is an award in the funding agency's own database, with its identifier.

This is not an allegation. Changing institutions is legal, ordinary and common, in both directions, and nothing here states or implies that any person did anything improper, broke any law, or failed any disclosure obligation. What the page shows is the public record: who was funded, by how much, where, and where they work now. Readers should draw conclusions about policy, not about people.

Liuqing Yang

Electrical engineering — wireless communications, ultra-wideband, connected vehicles

$4,377,013 in federal awards as principal investigator, at US institutions
10 NSF awards, 2006–2021

Held faculty appointments at three US public universities over roughly fifteen years and was principal investigator on ten National Science Foundation awards across them, including two CAREER grants — the NSF's award for early-career faculty it expects to become leaders in their field. Now Chair Professor and Acting Head of the Intelligent Transportation Thrust at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou).
