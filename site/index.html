The departments

One field, one institution, 2015–2022. Conservative by design — 80+ doctorates awarded, and 70%+ non-resident in at least six separate years. One unusual cohort cannot qualify.

These are federal records, not estimates.

Every figure in this table comes from IPEDS Completions — the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System, collected by the National Center for Education Statistics at the US Department of Education. Reporting is mandatory for every institution that participates in federal student aid programmes, and each institution reports its own degrees conferred. It is a census, not a sample or a survey of opinion.

“Non-resident alien” is a federal reporting category for students on temporary visas. It is recorded by the institution at the time the degree is awarded. Nothing on this table is inferred from names, ethnicity, or country of origin — those cannot be derived from this data and are not attempted here.

Method and limits

Everything here comes from federal files anyone can download — IPEDS Completions for degrees, the NSF NCSES Federal Support Survey for funding. The methodology page carries the full chain, and the bulk exports let you recompute any of it yourself.

  • Doctorates, not master’s. Cost figures cover research doctorates, where federal support concentrates. Master’s students mostly pay tuition; mixing the two would overstate the federal subsidy.
  • An intensity ratio, not a price tag. Federal obligations buy research — papers, instruments, facilities, staff — not degrees. A campus with a national laboratory ranks high for reasons unrelated to graduate training.
  • Composition, not displacement. Domestic STEM doctorates rose about a third between 2012 and 2025, roughly in step with the total. The seats were not fixed. What changed is who the growth was built for — and who pays for it.
  • Non-resident is a visa status at graduation, not ethnicity or country of origin. These files cannot be split by nationality, and they do not record who applied, was admitted, or was funded.
  • Reported, not inferred. Every figure on this page comes from a federal collection an institution was required to file: IPEDS Completions for degrees, the NSF federal support survey for funding. Elsewhere on this site, department-level and name-origin analysis is estimated from dissertation records, and that work is labelled as such. The two are never mixed: a name cannot establish a visa status, and this site does not claim otherwise.
  • Self-reported. Institutions file their own completions, and NCES publishes what they file. That makes the data authoritative and auditable rather than independently audited — which is why every case here also shows whether that institution has reported consistently over time.
