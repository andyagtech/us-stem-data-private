What this shows, and what it does not

What the data supports

These programs are supported by federal research funding, and their doctoral output goes overwhelmingly to students on temporary visas, consistently, for most of a decade. That is a question about the return on public investment, and it stands on its own.

It is also concentrated rather than general: the national non-resident share of research doctorates has been roughly flat since 2010. What changed is that particular departments became overwhelmingly non-resident while the average held still.

What it does not support

This is not evidence that Americans were displaced. Between 2012 and 2025, core-STEM research doctorates awarded to domestic students rose +32%, while the total rose +33%—they moved together. Master’s awards more than doubled. The number of seats was not fixed and domestic participation did not fall.

The data also cannot show who applied, who was admitted, or who was funded. Establishing that anyone was prioritised over anyone else would need admissions or funding records, which these files do not contain.
