Access authorisation

Two different regimes, often confused. Unescorted access to a commercial reactor requires NRC access authorisation under 10 CFR 73.56 — a background investigation and fitness-for-duty determination, not a security clearance, and not restricted by citizenship on its face. DOE work on weapons-adjacent programmes requires an L or Q access authorisation under 10 CFR 710, which IS a clearance and does require US citizenship. Most civil nuclear engineering falls under the first, not the second.

Method and limits

  • A CIP code is a field of study. Nobody in this data is observed working in the industry it is matched to.
  • The battery entry is a proxy built from materials programmes, because IPEDS publishes no battery category.
  • Shares are of degrees awarded, not of the current workforce. A doctorate awarded in 2016 may have left the country or the field.
  • 'Non-resident alien' is the IPEDS reporting category: a student on a temporary visa. Permanent residents are counted as domestic.
  • The restriction note states the law as written. It is not an observed hiring outcome. This data cannot show who was employed, under what authorisation, or whether they stayed.
  • Export control, access authorisation and a security clearance are three different things. Only a DOE L or Q access authorisation requires US citizenship.
