The grid is run by Americans. There are not many of them.

Every other page on this site asks who gets the degree. Ask that about the electrical grid and the answer is already settled: electrician credentials are 0.2% non-resident and lineworker credentials 0.3%. These are American jobs held by Americans.

So the question worth asking is not who. It is how many — and the country is about to ask this workforce to build more generation and more transmission than it has in fifty years.

Method and limits

This page counts credentials awarded. It does not count workers, and a credential is not a career.

  • IPEDS covers institutions that take Title IV federal aid. Much electrical and line work is learned in union or employer apprenticeships that award no IPEDS credential, so for the trades these counts are a floor on the pipeline rather than the pipeline.
  • A credential awarded is not a worker hired, and not a worker who stayed in the field.
  • Programmes are matched by CIP code. A college that teaches line work inside a broader electrical programme is counted under that broader code.
  • 'Non-resident alien' is the IPEDS category: a student on a temporary visa. Permanent residents count as domestic.
