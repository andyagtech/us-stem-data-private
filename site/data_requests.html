Data Asks

Every other page on this site reports what the public record says. This one reports where it stops.

Each ask below names something a federal office holds, or once published, that would answer a question this site currently cannot. It says what is missing, and it says which analysis is blocked. The figures beside each ask are read from this site's own built data when the page is rendered, so an ask cannot outlive the evidence for it.

19 of 31 are not requests for new collection. The agency already has the field on a form it already processes, or published it until recently and stopped. Those are disclosure decisions. The rest are marked, because a release decision and a budget request are different conversations.

Nothing here asks for anything that identifies a person. Several asks are deliberately for a tabulation rather than for record-level data, because the tabulation answers the question and the records would not be released.

Nobody is required to publish any of this

The gap this page describes starts before any federal agency. It starts with the fact that a university does not have to tell anyone what it awarded, to whom, or on whose money.

There is no federal rule obliging a university to publish a dissertation or a thesis. There is no rule obliging it to list the ones it has awarded. Each institution decides for itself, and the decision is often revisited department by department. There is no national register of awarded doctorates and no federal index of where a dissertation can be read. The largest single collection is held by ProQuest, which is a private subscription product.

So the record on this site was built one institution at a time, because there is no list to work from. Of the 106 institutions searched, 7 publish nothing the harvest could find, and 27 more are reachable only through a handle or DOI resolver rather than a browsable index. Several that do publish cover only recent years, or only some departments.

Coverage here is a floor, and the absence is not evenly spread. A university that posts less is not a university that awards less. Every institution-level figure on this site should be read as the smallest the true number can be, and the ranking between two institutions is partly a ranking of how much each chose to make public.

This is the first ask on the page, and it is the one the public most obviously already paid for.

How to read an ask

The badge is the part that decides who you are talking to.

Published before, then stopped

This was public and is not any more. The collection has not changed; only the release has.

No new collection

The agency holds this today, on a form or survey it already processes. Publishing it is a disclosure decision.

A tabulation would do it

The record-level data is sensitive or suppressed, but a tabulation at the agency's own thresholds would answer the question without releasing it.

Needs a new collection

Nobody holds this. It is a real cost and belongs in a different conversation from the rest of this page.

1. USCIS

H-1B cap-exempt status by employer

No new collection

Collected on
Form I-129, which records whether a petition is cap-exempt and on what ground.
Published now
National totals by exemption category in the Report to Congress. The H-1B Employer Data Hub names every employer and carries no cap field.
The request
The Employer Data Hub extract with two added fields, cap-exempt yes or no and the exemption ground, by employer and fiscal year, with approvals and denials.
Would settle
Which employers use the exemption, at what scale, and whether use is concentrated. Today the petitions filed under the affiliated-nonprofit ground cannot be attributed to any organization.

6. USCIS

L-1 and O-1 petitions by employer

No new collection

Collected on
Form I-129, the same form and system that produce the published H-1B Employer Data Hub.
Published now
Nothing by employer. Neither classification requires a labor condition application, so the Labor Department never sees them, and USCIS publishes employer hubs for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B only.
The request
Petitioner name and address for L-1A, L-1B, O-1A and O-1B, aggregated by petitioner and fiscal year, with approvals and denials. The H-1B hub's extract, run for four more classifications.
Would settle
Which companies transfer the most staff into the United States without a wage test, and whether O-1 growth sits in the industries most constrained by the H-1B cap.

10. USCIS

A stable employer identifier in the H-1B Employer Data Hub

No new collection

Collected on
Form I-129, which carries the petitioner's FEIN.
Published now
The employer name as filed, and nothing else. There is no key that survives a spelling change.
The request
Any stable per-employer identifier alongside the name. A hashed or truncated FEIN would do. The agency need not publish the number itself, only something that is the same across filings by the same body.
Would settle
Counting an employer at all. Cognizant files under eighteen spellings and the largest accounts for 22,287 of 50,994. Every employer total published anywhere, by anyone, is a floor, and the error is largest for exactly the biggest filers.

11. USCIS

Name the affiliated nonprofits in the Report to Congress

Published now

Sizing the bodies running the cap-exempt route. This site reaches for IRS Form 990 to do it, which gives revenue and headcount but says nothing about cap status, so the two can sit side by side and never join.

13. Department of Labor, Office of Foreign Labor Certification

A published column schema, and a diff when it changes

No new collection

Collected on
Not applicable. This is about the release, not the form.
Published now
Files whose column names change between years without notice. H_1B_DEPENDENT became H-1B_DEPENDENT in FY2025. FW_INFO_BIRTH_COUNTRY became FOREIGN_WORKER_BIRTH_COUNTRY in FY2020.
The request
A versioned schema published with each release, and a diff against the previous one.
Would settle
A whole category of silent error. A renamed column read by its old name returns no value, and a missing dependency flag reads as a firm that is never dependent. That is a wrong answer rather than a crash, and no downstream check can catch it reliably.
Note
Not a request for data. It would cost close to nothing and it is the cheapest item here.

28. DHS Student and Exchange Visitor Program

SEVIS by school, program, level and country of citizenship

No new collection

Collected on
SEVIS, continuously. Every F-1 and J-1 record carries the school, the program of study, the level and the country of citizenship, and is updated each term.
Published now
Quarterly totals by school and by country, in separate tables that cannot be crossed. The published school table does not carry country; the country table does not carry school.
The request

29. DHS Student and Exchange Visitor Program

Collected on

Published now
National totals. No employer appears in any published OPT table.
The request

15. DHS SEVP holds the authorisations; Treasury holds the payroll

Needs a new collection

Collected on

Nothing.

24. Department of Education; the National Center for Education Statistics would be the natural keeper of the register

A public deposit requirement for federally funded dissertations and theses

Needs a new collection

Collected on
Nothing. No federal rule obliges a university to publish a dissertation or a thesis, or to list the ones it has awarded. Each institution decides, and the decision is revisited department by department.
Published now
Whatever each university chooses to post. There is no federal register of awarded doctorates and no national index of where a dissertation can be read. ProQuest holds the largest collection and it is a private subscription product.
The request
A deposit rule with the grant money: a doctorate or a master's thesis completed with any federal support is lodged in a public repository within a year of the award, with the author, the institution, the department, the year and the advisor in a machine-readable record. No paywall. A single federal index of those records.
Would settle
Who is actually being trained, where, in what, and on whose money. This is the foundation under every other ask on this page, and it is the one the public most obviously already paid for.
Note
This is the gap that shaped the whole project. Building the dissertation record meant visiting institutions one at a time, because there is no list. Several publish nothing searchable at all. Coverage here is therefore a floor and the absence is not evenly spread -- a university that posts less is not a university that awards less.

How this page is built

The asks are declared once, in scripts/data_asks.py. Each one names the dataset that demonstrates the gap, and the builder reads the figure from that dataset when the page is rendered.

  • No figure on this page is typed. An ask whose evidence stops resolving fails the build, so the page cannot keep making a claim the data no longer supports.
  • Every gap described here was met while building something else on this site. None was looked for.
  • An ask is about publication, not about collection, unless it says otherwise. Where a field is already on a federal form, the page says so.
  • Nothing here is a criticism of the staff who publish these files. Most of these gaps are decisions taken years ago for reasons that were good at the time, and several would cost almost nothing to revisit.
