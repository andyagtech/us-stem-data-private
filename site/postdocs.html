Who holds the postdoc jobs

A postdoctoral appointment is the job an American doctorate leads to. It is the step between the degree and a faculty position or a research career, and roughly half of these posts are paid for with federal research money.

This page counts them by citizenship. A postdoc is counted as American here if they are a citizen or a permanent resident. Everyone else holds a temporary visa.

Americans are a minority of the postdoctoral workforce

Not at nine universities. Nationally.

58.3% of postdoctoral appointees in the United States hold a temporary visa. American citizens and permanent residents are 41.7% — a minority of the people doing postdoctoral research in their own country, in a workforce where about half the posts are paid for by the federal government.

This is the measured finding. What follows is what it means for the nine universities named in October.

The nine named universities

On 8 October 2026 the Department of Labor and the Department of Justice announced an investigation into J-1 visa use at nine universities, and said that about 61% of postdoctoral researchers and federally funded workers at those institutions are foreign nationals, against a national average of 38%.

The first figure checks out. Computed from the NSF survey, the nine come to 60.7%: 8,545 of 14,080 postdocs on a temporary visa.

The second figure does not match this survey. The national average on the same measure is 58.3%, not 38%. The two numbers count different things, and the difference decides what the story is.

The nine are not outliers. They are typical. They sit about 2.4 points above a national figure that is already over half.

That is the larger finding. If these nine were far above a low national average, this would be a story about nine universities and an enforcement action would address it. They are not. On this measure the American postdoctoral workforce is a minority of itself everywhere, and the nine named institutions look much like the rest of the country.

The survey reports how many postdocs are federally supported and how many hold a temporary visa. It does not report how many are both, so these two figures cannot be multiplied. A page that multiplied them would be inventing a number the survey does not contain.

Method and limits

What this counts, and what it cannot.

  • A postdoc counted as foreign holds a temporary visa. US citizens and permanent residents are counted as American.
  • The survey reports how many postdocs are federally supported and how many are foreign. It does not report how many are both, so the two figures cannot be multiplied.
  • Figures are summed from organisational units to the institution. A single GSS row is a school or department, not a university.
  • This counts postdoctoral appointees. Graduate students are a different population and are measured separately.
