Medical Immigration Pipeline

US MD-granting medical schools: enrollment growth, DACA students, and the limited domestic pipeline. Source: AAMC FACTS.
The medical school bottleneck. US MD programs admit ~23,000 new students per year — barely 40% of the ~55,000 who apply. While total enrollment has grown ~52% since 1980 (from 65,969 to 100,723), graduate output is still capped at ~21,000 per year. Combined with the residency-slot bottleneck, this creates one of the most constrained physician pipelines in the developed world. International medical graduates (IMGs) fill the gap — about 25% of practicing US physicians earned their MDs abroad.

Why this matters for the broader immigration story

Three structural facts about US physician supply:

  1. Seat scarcity: ~23,000 MD seats per year, ~6,400 DO seats. Combined US-trained output of ~30,000/year against a population of 340M.
  2. Residency cap (Match): Even US graduates compete for residency slots. About 40,000 NRMP positions annually — nearly half are filled by international medical graduates (IMGs) and US graduates of foreign schools.
  3. J-1 / H-1B physicians: Foreign-born physicians on J-1 visas serve a significant share of underserved-area care under Conrad 30 waivers. EVSP J-1 sponsorship data shows ~12,000 active foreign physicians on J-1 in any given year.

The medical immigration pathway is therefore very different from the STEM PhD pathway: tightly capped at the front (admissions), tightly capped in the middle (residency), and heavily reliant on foreign-born physicians (especially Indian and Pakistani) at the back end of the labor market.
