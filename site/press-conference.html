The press conference, with the data behind it

On 8 October 2026 the administration announced PERM suspensions for eight companies and a J-1 investigation into nine universities. The case rests on federal records that are difficult to carry in a speech. This site holds those records.

Each moment below quotes the recording and sets the measured evidence beside it. Press a timestamp to hear it said. Where the published record stops short, the page says what is missing and what would settle it — because the gaps are themselves part of the case.

Vice President JD Vance
They will say, maybe they'll put an advertisement in a small town newspaper... and they will use that lack of a response to then go and replace American workers.

The recruitment test

This is the PERM labour-market test: before sponsoring a worker for permanent residence an employer must advertise the role and show that no qualified American applied. The disclosure files record that a test took place. They do not record the advertisement, how many Americans applied, or why any were rejected — the Department of Labor holds that detail on the ETA-9089 and does not publish it. Releasing it would put evidence behind this passage. It is the first request on this site’s list.

Vice President JD Vance
If you are working as an H-1B visa in the United States of America, you're earning $20,000 less than an American citizen hired in the same position. If you were brought in by one of these foreign outsourcing firms, you're making $48,000 less.

The wage gap

The disclosure files publish the wage an employer OFFERS, not the wage paid, so the offered figures understate any gap rather than overstate it. What this site can show at scale is where the offers sit: the staffing firms named a moment later file overwhelmingly at the lowest statutory wage levels. The figure that would settle the question outright is a Treasury tabulation of W-2 wages against the prevailing wage for the same occupation and area. Treasury holds it. Nobody has published it.

The NSF Survey of Graduate Students and Postdoctorates puts the nine named universities at the share given from the podium. The same survey shows this is not confined to them: across every institution it covers, most postdoctoral appointments in the United States are held on a temporary visa. American citizens and permanent residents are a minority of the postdoctoral workforce nationally — in a workforce where roughly half the posts are paid for by federal research money. The nine are where the investigation starts, and the pattern runs through the whole system.

What the averages hide

Every share quoted from a podium is an average, and an average is the one statistic that cannot show an outlier. The national postdoc figure is 58.3%. The median institution is 60.9% — higher — because the largest universities pull the weighted average down. At the typical American institution the position is worse than the national number suggests.

How to read this page

The quotations are verbatim, taken from the published captions with their timestamps, so any one of them can be heard in context by pressing the time.

Six people spoke and each quotation names its speaker.

Every figure resolves from a federal dataset when the page is rendered. None is typed, and a figure that stops resolving fails the build rather than appearing as a dash. Each one links to the page where it can be checked at source.

Where the record stops, the page says so. Several of these claims cannot be settled from published data — not because they are wrong, but because the agency does not release the field. Those are listed as data requests, and they are the most useful thing this site can offer anyone working on this.

An investigation was announced. That is a fact with a date. Nothing here states that any institution or company broke a law.
