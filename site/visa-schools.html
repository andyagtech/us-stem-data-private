The schools that launder visas

At a growing number of American colleges, foreign students are most of the graduate enrolment. The school takes the tuition. It pays recruiting firms to fill the seats. It issues the paperwork for the student visa, and signs off on the work permits that come with it. Most of these schools also collect federal student aid.

Every step of that is lawful, and nothing on this page says otherwise. A college may enrol whoever it admits. Paying a recruiter is ordinary practice. Issuing an I-20 and recommending OPT is what a school certified by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program is authorised to do, and holding a Title IV agreement is a matter of public record. “Launder” is this site’s word for the pattern these records show. It is not a legal finding, and no school or firm named here is accused of breaking a law.

The student route is now bigger than the work visa

A graduate working on OPT or the STEM extension needs no new visa, no consular appointment and no employer petition. It is work authorisation on an F-1 already issued. Set that against H-1B visas issued at a consulate, and the two have changed places.

      What this adds up to

      These institutions take federal student aid Americans alone can use, and award most of their graduate seats to people who cannot.

      These 57 schools are not teaching Americans. Foreign students are most of their graduate enrollment, and at several of them it is nine in ten.

      They are funded anyway. 53 of them take federal student aid, drawing $388m in Pell grants and $618m in federal loans. Foreign students cannot receive either. That money is American students' aid, paid to institutions whose graduate programs are mostly not for them.

      What the money buys is a work visa. The degree is the product and the visa is why it sells, and the American taxpayer is underwriting the machinery.

      Method and limits

      • Enrolment is IPEDS Fall Enrollment part A: all graduate students (EFALEVEL 12, LSTUDY 3), non-residents from EFNRALT. Schools with fewer than 100 graduate students are left out, because a handful of students can swing their share.
      • “Takes part in federal student aid” means the school holds a Title IV Program Participation Agreement (IPEDS directory, OPEFLAG = 1).
      • Pell grants and federal loans are paid to the institution and applied to students’ tuition and fees first. Non-resident aliens are not eligible for either, so the dollars were awarded to the school’s eligible students.
      • Recruiting payments are as filed on each nonprofit’s IRS Form 990: the independent contractors paid more than $100,000 whose services are recruitment, and expense lines that name recruitment. For-profit schools and public universities do not file a 990.
      • Visa issuances are counts of visas, not people: a visa is re-stamped for travel.
