What a row does not mean

None of this is money broken down by citizenship, because no federal agency publishes it that way. IPEDS reports tuition revenue without regard to who paid it and the NSF reports obligations without regard to who they train. A large federal figure sitting beside a large non-resident share is two things measured in the same place, not a payment from one to the other.

Federal per doctorate is an intensity ratio, not a price. Those obligations fund research whose outputs include papers, instruments, facilities and staff, not only degrees. The column shows the scale of public research money moving through an institution relative to the doctorates it produces. It does not establish that the public paid that sum for any particular degree — and at the top of that ranking sit medical schools, where the money funds clinical research and the doctorates counted are a fraction of the degrees awarded. The two columns beside it say how large a fraction.

Non-resident is a visa category, not a birthplace. A naturalised citizen or a permanent resident is counted as domestic throughout, so every share here is a floor on the foreign-born proportion, never a ceiling.

Coverage is uneven and the table says so rather than filling gaps with zeroes. A blank federal column means the institution was not in the survey, not that it received nothing.

What this adds up to

An American applying to their own state university now applies to a program where they are the minority admitted.

The master's degree is where the money is, and it is where Americans have lost the most ground. At 121 institutions, most of the master's degrees awarded go to people who are not Americans.

These are not marginal places. They include public universities built by state legislatures, funded by state taxpayers, and founded to educate the people of those states. An American applying to a program at their own state university now applies to a program where they are the minority admitted.

Nothing in the law requires this and no one voted for it. It is the accumulated result of institutions following the money, with nobody counting the result until now.
