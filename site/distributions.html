A flat average, a moving distribution

The national non-resident share of US STEM research doctorates has barely moved in fifteen years. That single number is why the change is easy to miss: it describes almost no actual department. These three views take the average apart.

IPEDS Completions, research doctorates (award level 17), core-STEM CIP families. A department is one 4-digit programme at one institution — Electrical Engineering at Purdue, not "Engineering" at Purdue. Method and limits

0c · The longest view

IPEDS carries no citizenship breakdown before 1995, so the series above cannot start earlier. The National Science Foundation’s Survey of Earned Doctorates can: it is a census of every research doctorate awarded in the United States, and it reaches back to 1979.

NSF NCSES Survey of Earned Doctorates, all fields, by citizenship status at the time of the award. Not the same measure as the chart above — that one counts core-STEM doctorates at named institutions, this one counts all fields nationally, so the two are shown separately rather than joined into one line. Published five-yearly to 2004 and annually from 2009; where the data is five-yearly the line is dashed, and where it is annual it is solid. Chained across seven NCSES releases, newest winning any year they disagree on.

0b · The same picture at master’s level

The doctorate is the smaller, slower stream. The master’s is where the expansion actually happened — and where the raw counts move hardest.

Loading…

IPEDS Completions, award level 7, same eight core-STEM CIP families, 2008–2025. The series begins at 2008 because the CTOTALT/CNRALT residency columns do not exist in earlier files. Note the dip in 2022 and the jump after it: those graduates enrolled in autumn 2020, under the pandemic travel restrictions. It is a real cohort effect, not a reporting artefact — the number of reporting institutions moves smoothly across it (6,042 in 2022, 5,960 in 2023), and the swing is confined to the non-resident line while the domestic line keeps climbing.
